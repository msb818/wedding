# Guest Experience, Logistics & Colorado-Specific Notes

A foothills wedding is a destination wedding for almost all your guests
— even ones from Denver, because they're driving up a mountain and
sleeping near the venue. Treat it like one.

## The Colorado marriage license

This is unusually easy compared to other states. Don't stress about it.

- **Where:** Any Colorado county clerk and recorder's office. Jefferson
  County (Golden) or Denver County are most convenient. You don't have
  to apply in the county where the wedding happens.
- **Cost:** $30 (cash or card depending on county).
- **Both parties must appear in person** with photo ID.
- **No waiting period.** License is valid the moment they hand it to you.
- **Valid for 35 days.** For a 9/4/27 wedding, apply between **July
  31 and Sep 4, 2027**. Sweet spot is **mid-August**.
- **No blood test, no proof of residency, no premarital counseling
  required.**
- **No witnesses required** at the ceremony in Colorado.
- **Self-solemnization is legal in Colorado.** You and your partner can
  sign your own marriage license — no officiant needed. You can still
  have someone "lead" the ceremony as a friend, but the legal signing
  can just be the two of you. This is unique to Colorado and a handful
  of other states.

After the wedding, the officiant (or you, if self-solemnizing) returns
the signed license to the county clerk's office within **63 days**. The
state records the marriage. Order certified copies (~$1.25 each) for
name changes, insurance updates, etc.

## Altitude — talk about it on the website

This is the #1 most overlooked thing about a Front Range foothills
wedding. Your sea-level guests (East Coast, Texas, Southeast, anyone
from a coastal city) may feel altitude effects.

Common symptoms: headache, fatigue, mild nausea, shortness of breath
on stairs, trouble sleeping the first night, getting drunk faster than
expected. Usually mild, usually passes in 24–48 hours, occasionally
ruins someone's Friday.

**Put this in the wedding website:**

> A note for our out-of-town guests — our wedding is at ~7,000 feet
> elevation. Many people from lower elevations feel mild altitude
> effects on the first day (headache, fatigue, getting buzzed faster
> than usual). To help yourself adjust:
>
> - Drink more water than you think you need (start the day you
>   arrive).
> - Go easy on alcohol the first 24 hours.
> - Pack any usual headache medicine (Tylenol, ibuprofen).
> - Sunscreen — the sun is stronger up here than you expect.
> - Bring a layer for the evenings, it gets cold once the sun drops.
>
> If you have a heart or lung condition, talk to your doctor before
> the trip.

**In the welcome bag:** a bottle of water, a few packets of electrolyte
mix (LMNT or Liquid IV), a small Tylenol packet, a sunscreen stick or
single-use packet, a granola bar. Cost: ~$8–12/bag. Guests remember.

## Lodging

Set up hotel room blocks at **2–3 hotels at different price points**
in a central location, ideally Golden, Lakewood (DTC-adjacent), or
downtown Denver depending on venue. Most blocks are **courtesy
blocks** (no contractual obligation, hotel just holds rooms at a rate
until a deadline, ~30 days out) — these are the right kind. Avoid
**guaranteed blocks** where you're on the hook for unused rooms.

For a Labor Day Saturday in 2027, set up blocks no later than **summer
2026**. Denver hotels fill up.

A typical setup:
- 1 nicer hotel ($250–400/night) near the venue or in downtown Denver
- 1 mid-range hotel ($150–250/night)
- 1 budget option ($90–150/night) for friends and younger guests

Include addresses, the block code, and the booking deadline on the
website.

## Transportation

**Strongly recommend hiring shuttles** from your main hotel block(s)
to the venue and back. Foothills roads + alcohol + dark + unfamiliar
guests = the most preventable disaster of a foothills wedding.

Typical setup:
- One pickup from each hotel ~45 min before ceremony
- One return trip at end of cocktail hour (for early-leavers / older
  guests)
- One return trip at end of reception
- One late-night return trip 30 minutes after that

Cost: $1k–$3k for two shuttles for ~6 hours. Cheapest insurance policy
you'll buy.

Make it clear on the website: shuttles are provided, please use them.

## The wedding website

Use Zola, Withjoy, The Knot, or Joy. All free, all good. Build it
**before save-the-dates go out** (November 2026) and keep it updated.
Must include:

- Date and start time (ceremony start, not "wedding starts")
- Venue name and full address with a map link
- Hotel blocks with booking links / codes and the deadline
- Travel info: nearest airport (DEN), driving time, Lyft/Uber
  availability (often poor at foothills venues — note this)
- Shuttle info
- Altitude warning + what to pack
- Weather expectations
- Dress code (be specific — "cocktail attire, no jeans, comfortable
  shoes recommended for outdoor ceremony")
- Registry link
- RSVP page
- FAQ: kids welcome or not, plus-ones policy, what time to arrive,
  what to do in Denver Thursday/Friday, can guests stay Sunday/Monday
  (yes, Labor Day Monday is a holiday)
- Schedule of events (welcome happy hour Friday, ceremony Saturday,
  brunch Sunday if you're doing one)

## Recommendations for guests' weekend

People will fly in for a 3- or 4-day trip. Help them plan it. On the
website or in welcome bags, include a short "Things to do in Denver"
list — Red Rocks tour, hike at Mt. Falcon or Lookout Mountain, RiNo
breweries, the Denver Botanic Gardens, Coors Brewery in Golden, Mt.
Evans drive (one of the highest paved roads in the US, ~1.5 hr from
Denver). This costs you nothing and dramatically increases guest
satisfaction.

## Kids / plus-ones / dietary

Decide early. State clearly on the invitation.

- **Kids:** "We love your children, but we have chosen to make our
  wedding an adults-only event." Communicate via website FAQ, not
  invitation. Address the envelope to the adults only.
- **Plus-ones:** Default to allowing them for established couples
  (living together, engaged, married, long-term). Don't allow random
  dates for single guests at a tight-budget wedding. Be consistent.
- **Dietary:** RSVP form should capture dietary restrictions and
  allergies. Tell the caterer the totals 10 days out.

## Day-of guest experience checklist

- Ceremony starts on time (you set call time on the invitation 30
  minutes before actual start).
- Programs are nice but optional — many couples skip.
- Water stations at the ceremony, especially outdoor in CO sun.
- Pashminas / shawls in a basket for cold guests in the evening.
- Sunscreen station for outdoor cocktail hour (yes, really).
- Bug spray if your venue tends to mosquitoes after sunset.
- Clear signage: where's the ceremony, where's cocktails, where's
  reception, where are bathrooms.
- A guest book or alternative (photo Polaroid station, signed art
  print, audio guestbook).
- A late-night snack (sliders, tacos, fries, pizza) at 9:30 PM after
  people have been drinking and dancing. Universally beloved, fairly
  cheap, hugely memorable.
- An end-of-night send-off — sparklers (check venue's policy and CO
  fire restrictions in September), glow sticks, a "last dance" callout.
- A clear "the bar closes at 10:30, shuttles leave at 11" comms plan.

## Day-after brunch (optional)

If many guests are staying for Labor Day Monday, host a casual
Sunday morning brunch at a hotel or restaurant for whoever wants to
come. $25–40/head, no formality, casual clothes, two hours, come and
go. Massive ROI on the "people felt cared for" axis.

## The most underrated tip

**Eat breakfast and lunch on the wedding day.** Set an alarm. Have
someone in the bridal/groom suite responsible for putting food in
your hand at noon. You will not feel hungry — adrenaline does that.
You will also not eat at the reception — too many people to talk to.
If you don't eat lunch, you'll be drunk on a champagne toast at 6 PM
and falling asleep at 9. Eat the sandwich.
